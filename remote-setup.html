Share your shoot outside the studio

Let a client, art director or retoucher watch your Capture One session live from anywhere, on your own web address, through Cloudflare. It's free, your router stays closed, and everyone needs your viewing code to get in.

Which way suits you

Temporary link

Ready in a minute. No account, no domain.

A random address like https://calm-river-word.trycloudflare.com that changes every time you turn sharing on. Good for a one-off.

How →

Your own address

About 20 minutes, once. Free Cloudflare account and a domain you own.

A permanent address like https://view.yourstudio.com you can put in a booking email and reuse on every shoot.

How →

Quick option: a temporary link

  1. In the LfS Viewer window, open Remote Sharing in the sidebar and choose Temporary link.
  2. Check there's a Viewing code and click Save (or New Code, then Save).
  3. Switch on Share outside the studio (in the window, or from the menu bar icon). After a few seconds the address appears under it, with buttons to copy it or show a QR code.
  4. Send the address and the viewing code. The address only works while sharing is on, and next time it will be a different one.

Your own address, step 1: a Cloudflare account

Already have a Cloudflare account? Skip to step 2.

  1. Go to dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up and sign up with your email and a password.
  2. Open the confirmation email from Cloudflare and click the link in it.
  3. That's all. The Free plan covers everything in this guide.

Step 2: your domain on Cloudflare

The tunnel needs a domain that Cloudflare runs. Pick the case that matches yours.

Your domain is already on Cloudflare

You're done with this step: go to step 3.

You have a domain somewhere else (GoDaddy, Squarespace, Namecheap, Google/Squarespace Domains…)

Read this first This moves the whole domain's DNS to Cloudflare, not just the viewer. Your website and email keep working as long as their records come across (step 4 below). If someone else looks after your website or email, ask them first. If you'd rather not touch your main domain at all, buy a separate inexpensive one for the studio instead (next section).
  1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Domains and select Onboard a domain.
  2. Type your domain without www, for example yourstudio.com, and continue.
  3. Choose the Free plan.
  4. Cloudflare shows the DNS records it found. Compare them with your current DNS provider's list and add anything missing, especially your website (@ and www) and your email (MX and TXT records). Then continue.
  5. Cloudflare gives you two nameservers, such as ada.ns.cloudflare.com. Sign in where you bought the domain, find its nameserver settings, and replace the existing ones with Cloudflare's two. If the registrar has DNSSEC turned on, turn it off first; you can turn it on again through Cloudflare later.
  6. Wait until Cloudflare shows the domain as Active. It's often within an hour and at most a day; Cloudflare emails you when it happens. Carry on with step 3 meanwhile; step 4 needs it active.

You don't have a domain

  1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Domains (called Domain Registration in some dashboards) and choose to register, or buy, a domain. Cloudflare Registrar sells domains at cost, with no markup.
  2. Pick a name, for example yourname-studio.com, and complete the purchase. It's set up on Cloudflare straight away, with nothing to wait for.

Prefer a temporary link instead? See the quick option.

Step 3: create the tunnel and connect LfS Viewer

A tunnel is a private line from your Mac out to Cloudflare. LfS Viewer runs it for you; you only create it once.

  1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Networking › Tunnels and select Create a tunnel. (In older dashboards it's under Zero Trust › Networks › Tunnels; if you're asked for a connector type, choose Cloudflared.)
  2. Name it, for example LfS Viewer, and select Create Tunnel.
  3. Choose macOS as the operating system. Cloudflare shows an install command containing a long code that starts with eyJ: that's the tunnel's token. Copy the whole command.
    Don't run the command. LfS Viewer has its own copy of cloudflared and runs it for you. Treat the token like a password: anyone with it could stand in for your tunnel.
  4. In the LfS Viewer window, open Remote Sharing in the sidebar, choose Your domain, paste the command into Tunnel token and click Save. It keeps only the token, in your Mac's Keychain. If macOS asks whether LfS Viewer may use it, choose Always Allow.
  5. Check there's a saved Viewing code, then switch on Share outside the studio.
  6. Back in the dashboard, the tunnel shows as connected within a few seconds. Select Continue.

Step 4: publish your address

This tells Cloudflare which address leads to your tunnel. Your domain must be Active (step 2).

  1. In Networking › Tunnels, open your tunnel, then the Routes tab. Select Add route, then Published application.
  2. Subdomain: a short word such as view, live or studio. Use one word only; a.b.yourstudio.com needs a paid certificate. Domain: your domain. The full address appears under them, for example view.yourstudio.com.
  3. Leave Path empty.
  4. Service URL: copy this exactly, including http://:
    http://localhost:8797

    Plain http, not https: the line between Cloudflare and your Mac is already encrypted by the tunnel, and visitors get https from Cloudflare. The number is LfS Viewer's tunnel port (Settings › Remote sharing); it isn't your viewer's usual 8787.

  5. Leave Additional application settings as they are, and select Add route. Cloudflare creates the DNS record for you.
  6. In LfS Viewer, type the full address into Public address, for example view.yourstudio.com. The address then appears under the sharing switch, with Copy and QR code.

Step 5: test it and share it

  1. On your phone, turn Wi-Fi off so it really comes in over the internet, and open your address.
  2. You should see Live view and "Enter the viewing code the photographer gave you" (or whatever you chose under Settings › Visitors see you as). Enter the code: the live view opens. LfS Viewer now says "1 watching remotely", and the phone is listed under Viewers.
  3. Send people the address and the viewing code, ideally separately (the address by email, the code by text). The QR code button in LfS Viewer is handy for someone sitting next to you.
Who can do what Remote visitors can look, zoom and filter. They can rate and tag only if you allow it under Ratings & Tags (with a PIN, or for anyone). They are never treated as your Mac. To shut one person out, find their device under Viewers and click Remove…: it stays blocked, even with the right code, until you click Unblock. To start over with everyone, give the others a New Code: everyone using the old one has to enter the new one. Sign Out All Devices does the same for PINs too.

Keeping it running

  • The link works only while LfS Viewer is running and your Mac is awake. For long shoots, in System Settings: on a desktop Mac, open Energy and turn on Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off; on a laptop, open Battery › Options, turn on Prevent automatic sleeping on power adapter when the display is off, and keep it plugged in.
  • Sharing stays switched on: next time LfS Viewer and the viewer start, sharing comes back by itself. Turn on Open LfS Viewer at login in Settings if this Mac is your studio machine.
  • LfS Viewer notifies you if sharing drops for more than 15 seconds, and again when it's back.
  • Switch sharing off when the shoot is over. Your address then shows a Cloudflare error page until next time.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat it means and what to do
"Invalid service URL format" when adding the route Include the protocol: http://localhost:8797, not localhost:8797.
The dashboard keeps waiting for the tunnel to connect Paste the token into LfS Viewer and switch on Share outside the studio (step 3). Running the command yourself isn't needed.
LfS Viewer: "Cloudflare doesn't recognise this tunnel" The token is wrong, or the tunnel was deleted. Copy the install command again from the tunnel's page in the dashboard, click Remove next to the token in LfS Viewer, paste, Save.
LfS Viewer: "Can't reach Cloudflare" Check the Mac's internet. A strict office or venue firewall must allow outgoing connections on port 7844.
Visitors get Cloudflare Error 1033 or 530 The tunnel isn't connected: sharing is off, LfS Viewer isn't running, or the Mac is asleep.
Visitors get 502 Bad Gateway The tunnel is up but can't reach the viewer: the Service URL's port doesn't match LfS Viewer's tunnel port (Settings › Remote sharing), or it says https:// instead of http://.
"This address is for your own network. Point the Cloudflare tunnel at…" The Service URL points at the viewer's main port (8787). Change it to the tunnel port shown in that message.
An error that a DNS record for that name already exists, when adding the route That subdomain is already used in your DNS. Pick another word, or delete the old record under your domain's DNS › Records if you're sure it's unused.
The domain stays "Pending" in Cloudflare The nameservers at your registrar aren't Cloudflare's two yet, or DNSSEC is still on there. Fix it at the registrar and give it a little time.
Your address doesn't load at all ("server not found") The route isn't there yet, or the domain isn't Active. Check step 4, and the spelling in Public address.
macOS asks about the Keychain after an update Choose Always Allow. It's LfS Viewer reading the token you saved.

Still stuck? In the LfS Viewer window open Log in the sidebar and click Copy Log; the token never appears in it.

Extra protection, privacy and cost

  • Email codes instead of a viewing code (optional). Cloudflare Access can ask visitors for a one-time code sent to their email, letting in only addresses you list. Set it up in the dashboard under Zero Trust › Access › Applications (self-hosted, your address), free for small teams. Then you may turn off Ask for a viewing code in LfS Viewer. Keep the viewing code unless Access is set up.
  • See where visitors are (optional). LfS Viewer's Viewers panel shows each remote visitor's country. To see their city too, with a map link, turn on Add visitor location headers under your domain's Rules › Settings › Managed Transforms. It's rough: it comes from their IP address.
  • Privacy. Your Mac connects out to Cloudflare; nothing is opened on your router and your Mac's address isn't revealed. The pictures pass through Cloudflare's network on their way.
  • Cost. The Cloudflare account, the tunnel and the temporary link are free. Only a domain costs money, if you buy one.
  • Guessing. After 20 wrong codes from anyone in 10 minutes, remote unlocking pauses for everyone (devices in the studio aren't affected) and LfS Viewer notifies you. Consider a new code if that happens.
